Seven days is enough time to see a real cross-section of Morocco — but only if the route is planned honestly. A lot of itineraries floating around online quietly skip over how long the drives actually are, and travelers end up either exhausted from too much time in a vehicle or disappointed that they didn't get the "real desert" they were promised because the schedule never allowed for it. This is a version built around actual geography and actual drive times, so you know exactly what a week like this involves before you commit to it.

The route below runs [Marrakech](/destinations/marrakesh-the-red-city-of-morocco) → [Ait Benhaddou](/destinations/ait-ben-haddou-unesco-world-heritage-site) → Dades Gorge → [Merzouga](/destinations/merzouga-sahara-desert) (Sahara) → [Fes](/destinations/fes-fez-imperial-city-in-morocco), which is the classic and most efficient way to link the imperial cities with the desert without doubling back on yourself. If you need to return to Marrakech instead of ending in Fes, we've noted that alternative too.

## Day 1: Arrive in Marrakech

Settle in and get your bearings in the medina — this is not a day to over-schedule, especially if you've just landed after a long flight. A slow afternoon in the souks, a mint tea somewhere with a view over Jemaa el-Fnaa, and an early night sets you up well for the driving days ahead. If you have energy, the Bahia Palace and the Majorelle Garden are both manageable in a single afternoon.

## Day 2: Marrakech to Ait Benhaddou via the Tizi n'Tichka Pass

This is where the trip actually starts moving. The drive from Marrakech over the **[Tizi n'Tichka pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tizi_n%27Tichka)** — the high mountain road through the Atlas — takes roughly 4 hours to Ait Benhaddou, and it's a destination in its own right, not just a means of getting somewhere. See our [full guide to Ait Benhaddou and Ouarzazate](/blog/ait-benhaddou-ouarzazate-guide) for more on why this stretch of the route is worth slowing down for. The switchbacks, the sudden change in landscape from palm groves to bare rock and snow-capped peaks (in cooler months), and the Berber villages along the way are part of the experience.

Ait Benhaddou itself, the fortified clay ksar you'll likely recognize from film and television, is worth a couple of hours to walk through. Overnighting here rather than pushing further means you catch it in late afternoon and early morning light, when it's at its best.

## Day 3: Ait Benhaddou to Dades Gorge via Ouarzazate

A shorter driving day — around 3 to 4 hours — that still covers a lot of ground. You'll pass through **Ouarzazate**, Morocco's film-industry hub, before the road climbs into the dramatic red rock formations of the **Dades Gorge**. This is a good day to slow the pace: the gorge's switchback road and the valley's kasbahs reward unhurried exploring rather than a quick photo stop.

## Day 4: Dades Gorge to Merzouga

The longest driving day of the week, at roughly 5 to 6 hours depending on stops, but also the most anticipated: this is the day you reach the Sahara. The route typically passes through the **Todra Gorge** if you route slightly north first, or continues more directly through Erfoud toward Merzouga and the dunes of **Erg Chebbi**.

By late afternoon, you'll trade the vehicle for a camel for the trek out to camp — timed, ideally, to arrive as the dunes catch the last of the sun. This is the night that most people book the whole trip for: dinner around the fire, traditional music under a sky with essentially no light pollution, and a proper night's sleep in a desert camp.

## Day 5: Sunrise in the Dunes, Drive to Erfoud/Rissani, Toward Fes

Wake before dawn for sunrise over Erg Chebbi — genuinely one of the best moments of the week — before heading back by camel or 4x4 to Merzouga. From here, the route toward Fes begins, typically with a stop in **Rissani**, a historic trading town with one of the region's most authentic souks, less geared toward tour buses than Marrakech's. The full drive to Fes takes most of the day, roughly 7 to 8 hours, so this is a long one; some itineraries break it with an overnight in the Middle Atlas around **Ifrane** or **Azrou** instead of pushing straight through.

## Day 6: Explore Fes

A full day dedicated to Fes el Bali, the old medina and one of the largest car-free urban areas in the world. Between the tanneries, the Al-Qarawiyyin (one of the oldest universities anywhere), and the sheer density of the medina's alleys, a single day is enough to get a strong sense of the city, though Fes rewards travelers who come back for longer.

## Day 7: Departure or Return to Marrakech

If Fes is your endpoint, this is a departure day, possibly with a final morning in the medina before heading to the airport. If you need to loop back to Marrakech instead, budget a full driving day (roughly 7-8 hours, or consider a domestic flight to save time) rather than trying to combine it with sightseeing.

## Alternative: Looping Back to Marrakech Instead of Ending in Fes

If your flights are fixed out of Marrakech, the same route works as a loop by returning via a more direct road after Merzouga rather than continuing on to Fes — this trims a day of driving but means skipping Fes entirely. Which version makes sense depends on your flight arrangements, and it's worth deciding before you book anything rather than trying to change plans mid-trip.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is 7 days enough time to see Marrakech, the Sahara, and Fes?**
Yes, but it's a full week with real driving days built in — this isn't a relaxed beach-holiday pace. If you'd rather spend less time in transit, consider dropping Fes and looping back to Marrakech instead, or extending to 9-10 days.

**How long is the drive from Marrakech to the Sahara desert?**
Broken across two days with an overnight in Ait Benhaddou or Dades Gorge, as above, it's roughly 9-10 hours of total driving time to Merzouga — which is why most itineraries split it rather than attempting it in one day.
